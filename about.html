The Basics

What is a panty liner?

A panty liner is a thin, absorbent personal-hygiene product designed to be worn inside underwear. Unlike a sanitary pad, which is primarily designed for menstrual flow, panty liners are generally thinner and intended for lighter everyday needs.

Everyday freshness

Extra reassurance for spotting or residual discharge alongside other products.

Important: Panty liners are not a replacement for sanitary pads or other menstrual products when menstrual flow is heavy.

When to Use

Five everyday situations

Discharge

Vaginal Discharge

Some women experience normal vaginal discharge as part of their everyday cycle. A panty liner can provide an additional layer when you prefer to keep your underwear feeling cleaner and more comfortable.

If discharge has an unusual colour, strong smell, or is accompanied by itching, pain or other concerning symptoms, consult a qualified healthcare professional.
Spotting

Period Spotting

Panty liners can be useful for light spotting before, after or between periods. For heavier menstrual flow, use an appropriate menstrual product such as a sanitary pad, tampon or menstrual cup.

Backup

With Tampons & Menstrual Cups

Some users may prefer a panty liner as an additional layer when using tampons or menstrual cups, particularly when there is light spotting or residual discharge.

Leakage

Light Urinary Leakage

Panty liners may be used for very light urinary leakage. For heavier or persistent leakage, choose a product specifically designed for incontinence and consult a healthcare professional if needed.

Confidence

After Intercourse

Some people may prefer an additional layer of protection after intercourse due to temporary moisture or discharge.

Who Can Use

Who can use panty liners?

Panty liners are generally designed for people who want an additional layer in their underwear for light moisture, discharge or spotting. The choice of personal-hygiene product depends on individual needs and comfort. Yuroka™ is designed for everyday personal hygiene and is not intended to diagnose, treat or prevent any medical condition.
